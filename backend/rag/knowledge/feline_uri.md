# Feline Upper Respiratory Infection (URI / Cat Cold)

## Overview

Feline Upper Respiratory Infection (URI), commonly called a "cat cold," is a very common illness in cats, especially in kittens and cats that have been in shelters or multi-cat households. It affects the nose, throat, and sinuses. The majority of URIs in cats are caused by two main pathogens: Feline Herpesvirus-1 (FHV-1) and Feline Calicivirus (FCV). Secondary bacterial infections with Bordetella bronchiseptica and Chlamydophila felis are also common.

---

## Symptoms
section: symptoms

Common symptoms of feline upper respiratory infection include:
- Frequent sneezing (often in fits)
- Runny nose with clear, yellow, or green discharge
- Watery or goopy eyes (conjunctivitis)
- Congestion and labored breathing through the mouth
- Loss of appetite due to loss of smell
- Lethargy and decreased activity
- Mild fever (above 39.5°C / 103.1°F)
- Drooling or ulcers in the mouth (more common with Calicivirus)
- Squinting or keeping one eye closed

---

## Home Care Steps
section: treatment

If your cat shows mild URI symptoms, the following supportive care steps can help:
1. Keep your cat warm and comfortable in a quiet area away from drafts.
2. Use a clean, warm damp cloth to gently wipe away nasal and eye discharge several times a day.
3. Run a humidifier near your cat, or take the cat into a steamy bathroom for 10-15 minutes twice a day to loosen congestion.
4. Encourage eating by offering strong-smelling wet food, warmed up slightly to enhance its aroma.
5. Ensure fresh water is always available; dehydration is a serious risk.
6. Keep the sick cat separated from other cats in the household to prevent spreading the infection.

---

## When to See a Vet Immediately
section: emergency

Take your cat to the vet without delay if:
- The cat stops eating or drinking for more than 24 hours.
- Breathing becomes labored, the cat is breathing with its mouth open, or you can hear wheezing.
- Nasal or eye discharge turns thick, dark green, or yellow.
- The cat appears extremely lethargic, is unresponsive, or cannot hold its head up.
- Symptoms do not improve within 5-7 days of home care.
- A kitten under 6 months old or a senior cat over 10 years shows any URI symptoms.

---

## Treatment Options (Vet-Prescribed)
section: treatment

Vets may prescribe:
- **Antiviral eye drops** (e.g., Idoxuridine) for herpesvirus-related eye infections.
- **Antibiotics** (e.g., Doxycycline, Azithromycin) for secondary bacterial infections.
- **L-Lysine supplements** to help suppress herpesvirus replication.
- **Appetite stimulants** (e.g., Mirtazapine) if the cat refuses to eat.
- **Subcutaneous fluids** if the cat is dehydrated.

---

## Prevention
section: prevention

- Vaccinate your cat. The FVRCP vaccine protects against Feline Herpesvirus and Calicivirus.
- Keep your cat indoors to reduce exposure to infected cats.
- Wash hands thoroughly between handling cats if you have a multi-cat household.
- Disinfect food bowls, litter boxes, and bedding regularly.

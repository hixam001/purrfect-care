# Feline Obesity and Weight Management
source: Association for Pet Obesity Prevention

## Overview
section: overview

Obesity is the most common preventable disease in cats. Studies estimate that 50–60% of cats in developed countries are overweight or obese. A cat is considered overweight when it weighs 10–19% more than its ideal body weight, and obese when it weighs 20% or more above ideal. Obesity significantly shortens a cat's lifespan and dramatically increases the risk of diabetes mellitus, hepatic lipidosis, osteoarthritis, urinary tract disease, heart disease, and respiratory compromise. The most important thing to understand is that obesity in cats is a medical disease — not a character flaw or inevitable part of ageing.

## Assessing Your Cat's Weight
section: assessment

Because cats vary widely in frame size, scales alone are not sufficient. Use the Body Condition Score (BCS) system, a 9-point scale:

- **BCS 1–3 (Underweight):** Ribs and spine visible from a distance; no fat cover; prominent hip bones.
- **BCS 4–5 (Ideal):** Ribs easily felt with light pressure, with a very slight fat covering. Abdomen tucks up when viewed from the side. Waist visible from above behind the ribs.
- **BCS 6–7 (Overweight):** Ribs difficult to feel; moderate abdominal fat pad; loss of waist definition from above.
- **BCS 8–9 (Obese):** Ribs cannot be felt at all; heavy abdominal fat pad that may sway when the cat walks; no waist; fat deposits around the neck and limbs.

A simple check: stand above your cat and look down. You should be able to see a gentle indentation behind the ribs (the waist). If the cat is a rectangle or oval from above, they are overweight.

## Common Causes
section: causes

- **Overfeeding:** Free-choice (ad libitum) feeding is the single biggest cause of feline obesity. Cats are grazers by instinct but domestication and calorie-dense food has broken their natural satiety signalling.
- **Dry food diets:** Kibble is energy-dense, carbohydrate-heavy, and easy to overconsume. Cats are obligate carnivores not designed to eat high-carbohydrate diets.
- **Neutering:** Neutered cats have a significantly lower metabolic rate and increased appetite. Calorie requirements drop by approximately 25–30% after neutering.
- **Indoor sedentary lifestyle:** Cats evolved to hunt 8–12 small prey per day. Most indoor cats are vastly under-stimulated physically.
- **Multiple cat households:** Competitive eating or protection of food from other pets leads to overeating.
- **Age:** Metabolic rate declines with age; senior cats need fewer calories.

## Health Risks of Obesity
section: causes

- **Diabetes mellitus:** Obese cats are up to four times more likely to develop type 2 diabetes. Weight loss alone can cause diabetic remission in many cats.
- **Hepatic lipidosis (fatty liver disease):** A paradox — obese cats that stop eating for any reason rapidly develop life-threatening liver disease.
- **Osteoarthritis:** Every additional 100g of body weight increases joint loading significantly. Weight loss is the single most effective intervention for arthritic cats.
- **Urinary tract disease (FLUTD):** Obesity and a sedentary lifestyle increase the risk of bladder stones and urethral blockage.
- **Hypertension and heart disease:** Increased blood volume and cardiac workload.
- **Anaesthetic risk:** Obese cats are significantly harder and riskier to anaesthetise.
- **Reduced lifespan:** Studies in cats show that obesity reduces lifespan by 2–3 years.

## Weight Loss Guidelines
section: treatment

Weight loss in cats must be gradual — rapid weight loss triggers hepatic lipidosis, which is potentially fatal:

1. **Calculate ideal weight:** Have your vet determine target weight based on frame size.
2. **Target rate:** Aim for 0.5–1% of body weight loss per week. For a 6 kg cat aiming for 4.5 kg, that's ~30–60g per week.
3. **Calorie calculation:** Ask your vet for a daily calorie target. Most cats need 40–50 kcal/kg of ideal body weight per day for weight loss.
4. **Measure food precisely:** Use a kitchen scale or measuring cup. "Eyeballing" is the most common reason weight loss fails.
5. **Diet choice:** High-protein, low-carbohydrate wet food is ideal. Prescription weight-loss diets (Hill's Metabolic, Royal Canin Satiety) contain increased fibre and protein to maintain satiety.
6. **Feeding frequency:** Divide the daily allowance into 3–5 small meals — multiple small meals maintain metabolism better than one or two large meals.
7. **Treats:** Maximum 10% of daily calories. Include treat calories in the total.
8. **Never starve a cat** to lose weight — the risks of hepatic lipidosis are too great.

## Exercise and Environmental Enrichment
section: treatment

Exercise is important but cats cannot be walked reliably like dogs. Instead:
- **Interactive play sessions:** Twice daily, 10–15 minutes each. Use wand toys (feather wands, fishing rod toys) that mimic prey movement.
- **Puzzle feeders and food-dispensing toys:** Make the cat "work" for food, replicating hunting. This slows eating and provides mental stimulation.
- **Vertical space:** Cat trees, shelves, and climbing structures encourage natural climbing and jumping.
- **Hunting feeding system:** Divide the daily food ration into multiple small portions hidden around the home for the cat to find.

## When to See a Vet
section: emergency

Always consult a vet before starting a weight-loss programme. In particular:
- Rule out an underlying medical cause of weight gain (hypothyroidism, Cushing's disease, though both are rare in cats).
- Obtain a precise target weight and calorie prescription.
- Monitor with monthly weigh-ins to ensure the rate of loss is safe and effective.
- If the cat stops eating at any point during a weight-loss programme, this is an emergency — see a vet immediately.

## Prevention
section: prevention

- Adjust calorie intake immediately after neutering (reduce by 25–30%).
- Feed measured meals rather than free-choice.
- Choose high-protein, low-carbohydrate wet food as the primary diet.
- Weigh your cat monthly and plot the trend — early action is much easier than correcting established obesity.
- Provide daily interactive play regardless of age.

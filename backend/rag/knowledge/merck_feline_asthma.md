## Overview
section: overview

Tracheobronchitis is an acute or chronic inflammation of the trachea and bronchial airways that may extend into the lungs. It is more likely to occur in cats already affected by respiratory disease or a disorder of the lungs or airways. Causes include immune dysfunction, parasites, diseases of the mouth and pharynx, or chronic coughing related to heart or lung disease. Other causes include smoke inhalation and exposure to chemical fumes.

Bronchial asthma (allergic bronchitis) is a syndrome in cats with similarities to asthma in humans. Young cats and Siamese and Himalayan breeds are most often affected. The sudden development of asthma in older cats is extremely uncommon. A chronic cough in older cats is often due to pneumonia. If shortness of breath is severe, bluish mucous membranes (signaling a lack of oxygen in the blood) may be seen.

## Symptoms
section: symptoms

Signs of feline tracheobronchitis and asthma include:
- Spasms of coughing — most severe after rest or at the beginning of exercise
- Shortness of breath, wheezing, or labored breathing that may come and go
- Slight fever in acute bronchitis cases
- Bluish mucous membranes if oxygen levels drop severely
- A mild, persistent cough that can last several weeks even after the acute stage passes

The acute stage of bronchitis typically passes in 2 to 3 days, although the cough may persist for several weeks.

## Diagnosis
section: general

The veterinarian's diagnosis is made from the history, physical examination, and clinical signs and by ruling out other causes of coughing. Diagnostic tools include:
- Chest X-rays to visualize airway inflammation
- Bronchoscopy (endoscope to view the bronchial tubes)
- Collection of biopsy and swab samples for laboratory analysis

These diagnostic tests may be needed in cases where initial treatment is not effective.

## Treatment
section: treatment

Feline asthma is primarily treated with corticosteroids to reduce inflammation. Treatment options include:
- **Oral or inhaled corticosteroids** — the main treatment for feline asthma
- **Aerosol chambers and specialized inhalers** — available to administer inhaled medications in cats
- **Bronchodilators** — additional medications to open the airways
- **Antibiotics** — if a bacterial infection is also present (broad-spectrum)
- **Nebulization or steam therapy** — mist from a humidifier or hot shower helps loosen secretions, making them easier to cough up

In mild cases or those with a recent onset of signs, rest, warmth, and proper hygiene may be effective alongside treatment of the underlying disease.

---

## Prevention
section: prevention

It is important to avoid aerosolized products or allergens that may worsen the disease, including:
- Cigarette smoke
- Perfumes and aerosol sprays
- Pollens and molds
- Household dust and dusty cat litters

Switching to a low-dust or dust-free cat litter can significantly help cats with asthma. Keep the cat's environment clean and well-ventilated.
